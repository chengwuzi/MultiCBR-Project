一、Abstract

Bundle recommendation integrates bundle-level feedback and item-level information to model users' preferences for sets of items. Recent methods learn complementary views from user-bundle (U-B) interactions, user-item behavior, and bundle composition, and combine them through representation fusion and contrastive learning. Despite this progress, two challenges remain: noisy U-B interactions can spread misleading signals through graph propagation, while sparse U-B feedback limits preference learning for users with few bundle interactions. To address these challenges, we propose ReCoBR, a framework that combines diffusion-driven graph reconstruction with cross-view contrastive learning. We develop a U-B graph denoising pipeline that integrates pretraining, user-conditioned latent diffusion, and graph reconstruction. Starting from pretrained user and bundle embeddings, we refine each user's aggregated historical bundle representation through conditional denoising and use the refined preference to re-score observed interactions and reconstruct the U-B graph. Within this pipeline, a bundle-set consistency loss complements preference reconstruction by encouraging agreement with the user's historical bundle representations. We further introduce pre-fusion cross-view contrastive learning anchored in the reconstructed U-B view. On both the user and bundle sides, the objectives connect U-B representations with their user-item and bundle-item counterparts before fusion, enabling behavioral and compositional information to support preference learning under sparse U-B feedback. Experiments on three public benchmarks show that ReCoBR outperforms the compared baselines.

二、Introduction

Bundle recommendation (BR) aims to match users with sets of related items, such as coordinated outfits and music playlists \cite{ma2022crosscbr,sun2024intent}. In these applications, the recommendation decision concerns a collection as a whole, so identifying attractive individual items alone is insufficient for determining which bundle a user will choose \cite{chen2019dam}. BR therefore needs to account for both users' choices of complete bundles and information about their constituent items. In this work, we focus on ranking predefined bundles using historical interactions and bundle composition.

This task draws on three relations: user-bundle (U-B) interactions, user-item (U-I) interactions, and bundle-item (B-I) affiliations \cite{chang2020bgcn,ma2024multicbr}. U-B interactions provide direct feedback on complete bundles, U-I interactions reflect users' behavior toward individual items, and B-I affiliations describe bundle composition. Modeling these relations as separate views allows a recommender to combine bundle-level collaborative evidence with item-level behavioral and compositional information.

Research on BR has progressively developed ways to combine this evidence. Early approaches use attention to aggregate constituent items and jointly model bundle and item interactions \cite{chen2019dam}, while graph neural networks (GNNs) extend representation learning to higher-order connections among users, bundles, and items \cite{chang2020bgcn}. Contrastive learning (CL) further connects different views: CrossCBR aligns separately learned bundle-view and item-view representations through cross-view CL \cite{ma2022crosscbr}, whereas MultiCBR models all three relations and applies CL to their fused representations \cite{ma2024multicbr}. DGMAE takes a complementary approach by distilling U-B knowledge into item-view representation learning \cite{ren2023dgmae}. These developments establish the value of exploiting auxiliary information for bundle ranking. The effectiveness of this cooperation nevertheless depends on the reliability of the U-B connections used for propagation and on how auxiliary evidence supports preference learning when direct bundle feedback is limited. These considerations motivate two challenges.

Challenge 1: Noise in user-bundle interactions. Observed implicit feedback does not always faithfully reflect user preferences \cite{wang2021adt}. In graph-based BR, such feedback also determines the connections used for neighborhood aggregation, allowing misleading signals to influence the representations used for bundle ranking \cite{wu2021sgl}. Since each connection concerns only one bundle, the user's broader bundle history offers a context for assessing its preference relevance. Simply reducing the number of connections does not establish which ones should be retained. The challenge is to use this historical context to refine the propagation graph while preserving information relevant to the user's bundle preferences.

Challenge 2: Sparse user-bundle feedback. For users with few bundle interactions, direct evidence of preferences for complete bundles is limited \cite{chen2019dam,ma2022crosscbr}. U-I behavior and B-I composition offer complementary information, but item-level preferences do not necessarily translate into preferences for a whole bundle \cite{ren2023dgmae}. Likewise, knowing which items constitute a bundle does not establish a particular user's interest in that bundle. The challenge is therefore to learn associations that allow item-level behavioral and compositional information to support personalized bundle preferences when direct U-B evidence is limited.

To address these challenges, we propose ReCoBR (Reconstruction and Contrastive Learning for Bundle Recommendation), a framework combining diffusion-driven U-B graph reconstruction with pre-fusion cross-view CL. For graph reconstruction, we first pretrain user and bundle embeddings on observed U-B interactions and aggregate each user's historical bundle embeddings into a latent preference vector. Following the diffusion denoising formulation \cite{ho2020ddpm,wang2023diffrec}, we train a denoiser to recover this vector from Gaussian perturbations, conditioned on the pretrained user embedding. A bundle-set consistency loss (SetLoss) complements the reconstruction objective by encouraging average directional agreement with individual historical bundle representations, connecting preference recovery to the similarity measure used for interaction scoring. The trained denoiser then iteratively refines the historical preference vector, which is used to re-score observed U-B interactions and retain high-scoring connections for graph propagation.

Building on the reconstructed U-B view, we introduce cross-view contrastive objectives to strengthen preference learning under sparse bundle feedback. The recommendation backbone learns user and bundle representations from the reconstructed U-B graph and the U-I and B-I relations, then fuses the view-specific representations for prediction. Before fusion, we pair each U-B representation with its U-I and B-I counterparts on both the user and bundle sides, using the reconstructed U-B view as their common reference. These objectives encourage agreement for the same entity across paired views while distinguishing different entities, providing additional learning signals from item behavior and bundle composition. Graph reconstruction thus refines the connections used for bundle-level propagation, while cross-view learning connects the resulting representations to auxiliary evidence.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

(1) We propose a diffusion-driven U-B graph reconstruction pipeline that combines collaborative pretraining, user-conditioned latent denoising, and a bundle-set consistency constraint to refine the observed connections used for recommendation.

(2) We introduce pre-fusion cross-view contrastive learning anchored in the reconstructed U-B view, linking U-B representations with their U-I and B-I counterparts on both the user and bundle sides to strengthen preference learning under sparse bundle feedback.

(3) Experiments on three public benchmarks demonstrate ReCoBR's effectiveness and clarify the roles of graph reconstruction and cross-view learning through ablations, interaction-selection comparisons, and user-group analyses.
