Diffusion-Driven Graph Reconstruction and Cross-View Contrastive Learning for Bundle Recommendation

Abstract

Bundle recommendation integrates bundle-level feedback and item-level information to model users' preferences for sets of items. Recent research has integrated user-bundle (U-B) interactions, user-item behavior, and bundle composition into a common multi-view framework that combines graph-based modeling, representation fusion, and contrastive learning. Despite this progress, two challenges remain: (1) noisy U-B interactions can spread misleading signals through graph propagation; (2) sparse U-B feedback limits preference learning for users with few bundle interactions. To address these challenges, we propose ReCoBR, a framework that combines diffusion-driven graph reconstruction with cross-view contrastive learning. ReCoBR pretrains user and bundle embeddings, learns a user-conditioned latent diffusion model to refine aggregated historical bundle representations, and uses the refined preferences to re-score observed interactions and reconstruct the U-B graph. To adapt the denoising process to bundle recommendation, we introduce a bundle-set consistency loss that complements preference reconstruction by encouraging agreement between the refined preference and historical bundle embeddings. The reconstructed U-B view then anchors cross-view contrastive learning before representation fusion. User-side and bundle-side objectives connect U-B representations with their user-item and bundle-item counterparts, allowing behavioral and compositional information to support preference learning under sparse U-B feedback. Experiments on three public benchmarks show that ReCoBR outperforms the compared baselines.


1 Introduction

Bundle recommendation (BR) aims to match users with collections of related items, such as music playlists and fashion outfits [1,2]. To rank such collections, a recommender can draw on users' previous choices of complete bundles as well as their interactions with individual items. These observations provide complementary evidence: bundle choices reflect interests at the collection level, whereas item interactions indicate which components may appeal to the user. Bundle composition links these sources by identifying which items belong to each predefined bundle, allowing item-level interests to inform bundle preference estimation.

Early approaches integrate the two types of behavior through joint learning. DAM jointly models user-bundle (U-B) and user-item (U-I) interactions, using attention to aggregate constituent-item representations into bundle representations [1]. Graph-based approaches further exploit the collaborative structure formed by these interactions. BGCN, for example, propagates information on the U-B and U-I graphs to learn bundle-view and item-view representations, respectively, and uses bundle-item (B-I) affiliations to connect items to bundles [3]. This graph formulation captures higher-order collaborative information while retaining two perspectives on users' bundle preferences.

Learning separate views also raises the question of how they can inform one another. CrossCBR addresses this question through cross-view contrastive learning, encouraging agreement between the representations of the same user or bundle across the bundle and item views [2]. MultiCBR extends the formulation to three views by encoding U-B interactions, U-I interactions, and B-I affiliations separately [4]. It combines these representations before prediction, allowing information from different views to interact in bundle scoring, and applies contrastive learning to the fused representation.

Cross-view learning enriches the information available for bundle recommendation, but observed U-B interactions still determine the bundle-view graph and provide ranking supervision. This dependence presents two challenges when the feedback is unreliable or scarce.

Challenge 1: Noise in user-bundle interactions. Implicit feedback can include interactions that do not faithfully reflect users' preferences [5]. When these interactions become edges in the U-B graph, neighborhood aggregation can spread their influence into the representations used for bundle ranking [6]. Graph refinement therefore calls for a personalized criterion for deciding which observed interactions to retain for propagation. The user's historical bundles provide a context for this decision: each interaction can be evaluated in relation to a representation of the user's overall bundle preferences. The challenge is to construct such a representation and use it to select propagation connections that support preference learning.

Challenge 2: Sparse user-bundle feedback. Even reliable feedback can provide limited evidence when a user has interacted with only a few bundles. U-I behavior and B-I composition offer additional information about the user's item-level interests and the contents of candidate bundles. However, interest in a constituent item does not necessarily imply preference for the complete bundle [7]. The value of this auxiliary information therefore depends on how it is related to bundle-level choices. In the three-view formulation above, contrastive learning regularizes the fused representation. We examine whether additional constraints between the U-B view and each auxiliary view before fusion can strengthen preference learning for users with limited bundle feedback.

To address these challenges, we propose ReCoBR (Reconstruction and Contrastive Learning for Bundle Recommendation), which couples diffusion-driven U-B graph reconstruction with cross-view contrastive learning. For graph reconstruction, we first pretrain user and bundle embeddings on the U-B graph [8] and aggregate each user's historical bundle embeddings into a preference representation. A latent diffusion model learns to recover this representation from a perturbed version, using the pretrained user embedding as a personalized condition [9,10]. To support subsequent scoring against individual bundles, a bundle-set consistency loss encourages directional agreement between the recovered preference and historical bundle embeddings. We then apply the trained denoiser to iteratively refine the historical preference representation, re-score observed interactions, and retain the highest-scoring connections for graph propagation. The original U-B interactions continue to provide ranking supervision.

The reconstructed graph forms the U-B view of a three-view recommender, alongside the U-I and B-I views [4]. To connect the auxiliary information directly to bundle-level learning, we introduce U-B–U-I and U-B–B-I contrastive objectives on both the user and bundle sides before representation fusion. These objectives jointly optimize the paired representations, using the reconstructed U-B view as their common reference. They complement the backbone's representation fusion and post-fusion contrastive learning, providing additional learning signals for users with limited bundle histories. ReCoBR thus combines preference-based selection of propagation connections with cross-view learning from behavioral and compositional evidence.

Our main contributions are as follows:

• We develop a diffusion-driven U-B graph reconstruction framework that integrates pretraining, user-conditioned historical preference refinement, and observed-interaction selection, supplemented by a bundle-set consistency objective.
• We introduce cross-view contrastive objectives that use the reconstructed U-B view as a common anchor for user-side and bundle-side learning from auxiliary views before fusion.
• Experiments on three public datasets demonstrate ReCoBR's recommendation improvements and examine the contributions of graph reconstruction and cross-view learning through ablations, interaction-selection comparisons, and user-group analysis.
