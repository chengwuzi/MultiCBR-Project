Diffusion-Driven Graph Reconstruction and Cross-View Contrastive Learning for Bundle Recommendation

Abstract

Bundle recommendation aims to match users with collections of related items. Recent approaches combine bundle-level interactions, item-level behavior, and bundle composition through multi-view graph learning and contrastive objectives. However, direct user-bundle (U-B) feedback can be noisy and sparse: unreliable connections can distort graph propagation, while limited bundle histories constrain personalized preference learning. To address these challenges, we propose ReCoBR (Reconstruction and Contrastive Learning for Bundle Recommendation). ReCoBR combines U-B pretraining with user-conditioned latent diffusion to refine representations of users' historical bundle preferences, then uses the refined representations to re-score observed interactions and reconstruct the propagation graph. A bundle-set consistency loss encourages directional agreement between recovered preferences and historical bundle embeddings. The reconstructed U-B view then serves as a common anchor for cross-view contrastive learning before representation fusion. By connecting this view to user-item and bundle-item views on both the user and bundle sides, ReCoBR draws on behavioral and compositional information to supplement limited bundle-level feedback. Experiments on three public datasets demonstrate that ReCoBR consistently outperforms the compared baselines.


1 Introduction

Bundle recommendation (BR) aims to recommend collections of related items that match users' preferences, with applications such as fashion outfits and music playlists [1,2]. These applications involve selecting items as a whole: an outfit brings together clothing for a coordinated look, while a playlist groups music for a shared theme or listening context. Modeling bundle preferences therefore involves both users' choices of complete bundles and their interests in constituent items. We study the ranking of predefined bundles using historical interactions and bundle composition information.

To capture these complementary sources of preference, existing methods model user-bundle (U-B) interactions together with user-item (U-I) interactions and bundle-item (B-I) affiliations. U-B interactions provide direct evidence of bundle choices, whereas U-I and B-I relations supply behavioral and compositional information at the item level. Early methods combine attentive item aggregation with joint learning of bundle and item interactions [1], and graph-based methods further capture collaborative patterns through neighborhood propagation [3]. Subsequent work strengthens cooperation between the resulting views. CrossCBR aligns bundle-view and item-view representations through cross-view contrastive learning [2]. MultiCBR separately encodes the U-B, U-I, and B-I relations, fuses their representations, and applies contrastive learning in the fused space [4].

These advances establish the value of multi-view learning, but the information available across views does not remove the limitations of the underlying U-B feedback. As the direct record of complete-bundle choices, this feedback shapes both the propagation structure of the U-B graph and the evidence available for learning each user's bundle preferences. The reliability of these interactions and the amount of feedback available for each user therefore raise two challenges.

Challenge 1: Noise in user-bundle interactions. Implicit feedback can contain interactions that do not faithfully reflect users' interests [5]. When such interactions form graph connections, neighborhood aggregation can spread their influence into user and bundle representations [6]. This motivates reassessing observed connections in the context of each user's bundle history. Historical bundle representations summarize the collections the user has chosen and provide a personalized reference for interaction assessment. However, this reference is itself derived from imperfect feedback. The difficulty is to reassess observed connections using this imperfect historical context while preserving preference-relevant information for graph propagation.

Challenge 2: Sparse user-bundle feedback. For users with few bundle interactions, direct feedback provides limited evidence for personalized learning. Auxiliary relations offer additional information, but interest in individual items does not necessarily imply interest in a particular bundle [7]. CrossCBR and DGMAE demonstrate the benefits of relating bundle-level knowledge to item-level learning through contrastive objectives and distillation, respectively [2,7]. In MultiCBR, three-view fusion and contrastive regularization in the fused space support joint preference modeling [4]. Building on this framework, we examine how explicit associations between the U-B view and both auxiliary views before fusion can complement fused-space learning when direct bundle feedback is limited.

To address these challenges, we propose ReCoBR, a framework that connects diffusion-driven U-B graph reconstruction with cross-view contrastive learning. The graph reconstruction module begins by pretraining user and bundle embeddings on U-B interactions with LightGCN [8]. We aggregate each user's historical bundle embeddings into a preference representation. Inspired by diffusion-based recommendation [9,10], we train a denoiser to recover this representation from Gaussian perturbations, using the pretrained user embedding as a personalized condition. A bundle-set consistency loss supplements the reconstruction objective by encouraging directional agreement with the historical bundle embeddings used for subsequent scoring. We then iteratively refine the preference representation, re-score the user's observed interactions, and retain the highest-scoring connections to reconstruct the U-B graph. The reconstructed graph determines propagation connections, while the original interactions continue to provide ranking supervision.

The cross-view module uses the reconstructed U-B view as a common anchor for contrastive pairing with the U-I and B-I views before fusion. On both the user and bundle sides, the U-B–U-I and U-B–B-I objectives jointly optimize paired representations by encouraging agreement for the same entity while distinguishing different entities. These constraints complement the three-view backbone's representation fusion and post-fusion regularization [4], allowing auxiliary behavioral and compositional information to participate directly in learning bundle-level representations. The two modules work together through the U-B view: graph reconstruction refines its propagation connections, and cross-view learning connects its representations to additional evidence beyond each user's limited bundle history.

Our main contributions are as follows:

• We develop a diffusion-driven U-B graph reconstruction framework that integrates pretraining, user-conditioned historical preference refinement, and observed-interaction selection, supplemented by a bundle-set consistency objective.
• We introduce cross-view contrastive objectives that use the reconstructed U-B view as a common anchor for user-side and bundle-side learning from auxiliary views before fusion.
• Experiments on three public datasets demonstrate ReCoBR's recommendation improvements and examine the contributions of graph reconstruction and cross-view learning through ablations, interaction-selection comparisons, and user-group analysis.
